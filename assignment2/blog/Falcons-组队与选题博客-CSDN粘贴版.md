### 1.3 Team Vision

We chose the Sports Buddy platform because of a real pain point: **students want to exercise, but it is hard to find partners who match in time, skill and location, and who show up.**

Our vision can be summed up in three words — **"约得到，约得好，约得准"** — buddy up, easily; buddy up, well; buddy up, precisely:

1. **Buddy up, easily (约得到)**: with activity creation, browsing and sign-up, anyone who wants to exercise can always find someone to go with, turning "wanting to exercise" into "actually exercising".

2. **Buddy up, well (约得好)**: the reliability score and mutual reviews reduce no-shows and skill mismatches, so the small frustrations of group sports become fewer and fewer.

3. **Buddy up, precisely (约得准)**: smart matching and one-line activity creation connect you with the right partner — the right time, the right skill level, the right place.

We don't pursue being large and all-inclusive; instead we **first make one campus truly usable**, build a product that "really works and people want to use", and later extend it to more universities, societies and communities.

## 2. Project Introduction

### 2.1 Background & Reasons

Students around us often go through this: wanting to play ball, run, or hike, but finding no one to go with; posting an invitation in a group chat, only to have it buried by other messages in minutes; finally getting enough people together, but the skill gap is too wide, or someone flakes at the last minute and the activity falls apart. We want to build a lightweight, reliable tool that turns "wanting to exercise" into "actually exercising."

### 2.2 Project Description

**Sports Buddy** — a sports matchmaking platform for university students, helping people who want to exercise quickly find partners who match in time, skill level and location, and who can be relied on.

| Feature | Description |
| --- | --- |
| Create Activities | Post sports activities with time, location, headcount and skill requirements |
| Sign Up | Browse and join activities of interest |
| Check In | On-site check-in to record participation |
| Review | Rate each other after activities to build reputation |
| Reliability Score | Compute a reliability score from attendance and reviews to reduce no-shows |
| Smart Matching | Recommend suitable activities and partners by time, location and skill |
| One-line Creation | Create an activity quickly with a single natural-language sentence |
| Weather Tips | Provide activity suggestions based on weather |

**Tech Stack**: [To be added — to be decided in the design phase]

### 2.3 NABCD Analysis

**N — Need**

People want to exercise but can't find partners; group-chat invitations get buried and information is scattered; when people do get together, skill gaps are wide and someone often flakes. University students lack a lightweight, reliable tool for organizing sports.

**A — Approach**

Build a sports matchmaking platform for university students: start with activity creation, sign-up, check-in and reviews to stabilize the core flow; then add a reliability mechanism, smart matching and one-line activity creation to upgrade "can find someone" into "can find the right someone."

**B — Benefit**

Make it easier for students who want to exercise to find partners; reduce small frustrations like no-shows and skill mismatches; make the sports atmosphere on campus a bit more lively.

**C — Competitors**

| Competitor | Strength | Our Difference |
| --- | --- | --- |
| WeChat / QQ group chats | Zero barrier, everyone uses them | Messages get buried, no reliability or matching; we focus on sports matchmaking |
| Campus forums / confession walls | Wide reach | Messy information, no sign-up / check-in closed loop |
| Keep and other fitness apps | Professional, huge user base | Focus on personal workout logging, don't solve the "finding people" problem |
| Generic companion apps | [To be added after research] | [To be added after research] |

**D — Delivery**

Phase 1 delivers a minimum viable product (activity creation, sign-up, check-in, reviews) that students on one campus can actually use. Phase 2 adds reliability scores, smart matching and one-line creation. Phase 3 iterates based on trial feedback and promotes the product to other universities and clubs. Start with everyday campus sports — badminton, basketball, running, gym — then extend to class/society group activities, weekend hikes and running clubs, and later to neighboring universities and communities.

## 5. Performance Assessment

### 5.1 Objective

Make everyone's contribution visible, align contribution with responsibility, and promote collaboration rather than comparison.

### 5.2 Dimensions & Weights

| Dimension | Weight | Basis of Evaluation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Task Completion & Quality | 40% | Whether board tasks are completed on time and pass acceptance |
| Code / Document Quality | 20% | Conclusions of code reviews and document reviews |
| Collaboration & Communication | 15% | Meeting participation, response timeliness, willingness to help others |
| Attendance & Punctuality | 10% | Meeting attendance, on-time delivery |
| Peer Review | 15% | Anonymous peer review at the end of each iteration |

**Assessment Cycle**: assessed once after each iteration; the final individual contribution ratio is based on the cumulative results across all iterations.

**Data Sources**: commit history, merge requests and review records in the code repository; the task board; meeting sign-ins; peer review forms.

### 5.3 Scoring Method

Individual score = sum of each dimension's score × its weight, out of 100. Scores fall into four tiers:

- 90 and above: Excellent
- 75–89: Good
- 60–74: Pass
- Below 60: Needs Improvement

Results are announced and briefly reviewed at the meeting after each iteration.

### 5.4 Fairness & Anti-Gaming Mechanisms

- Commit count is not used as a standalone metric, to avoid splitting commits for the sake of numbers.
- Workload differs across roles, so tasks are weighted by difficulty and effort instead of being compared by sheer quantity.
- Peer review is anonymous, and the average is taken after dropping the highest and lowest scores.

### 5.5 Feedback & Appeal

- Members who disagree with their assessment may appeal to the team lead within three days of the announcement; the whole team will discuss and re-evaluate if necessary.
- A member rated "Needs Improvement" for two consecutive iterations will have a one-on-one talk organized by the team lead to jointly analyze the causes and adjust task arrangements.

### 5.6 Application of Results

The performance score serves as an important reference for the final course grade.
